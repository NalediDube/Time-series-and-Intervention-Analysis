# 1.1 Purpose of the Study 
The retail trade sector serves as a fundamental barometer of macroeconomic vitality, 
consumer confidence, and household financial resilience. In South Africa, retail trade 
contributes substantially to gross domestic product (GDP) and formal employment. 
However, monthly retail demand is inherently volatile, exhibiting strong seasonal cycles, 
inflationary pressures, and sensitivity to exogenous shocks. 
In March 2020, in response to the emerging global COVID-19 pandemic, the South African 
government declared a National State of Disaster (15 March 2020) and enacted a 
nationwide Level-5 Lockdown taking effect on 26 March 2020. This unprecedented 
regulatory intervention initially triggered a wave of precautionary consumer stockpiling in 
mid-March 2020, followed immediately by the mandatory shuttering of all non-essential 
commercial operations, strict movement restrictions, and the prohibition of alcohol and 
tobacco sales across April 2020. 
While conventional descriptive statistics reveal an abrupt fluctuation in economic output 
during this period, naive regression models fail to distinguish between transient, short
term shocks and permanent structural shifts, and fail to account for strong seasonal 
autocorrelation. The purpose of this study is to implement a rigorous Interrupted Time 
Series Analysis (ITSA)—also termed Intervention Analysis (Box & Tiao, 1975)—anchored 
at the March 2020 policy intervention point (𝑇0 = March 2020) to isolate, model, forecast, 
and quantify the precise empirical consequences of the COVID-19 lockdown on South 
African monthly consumer retail sales volume.
